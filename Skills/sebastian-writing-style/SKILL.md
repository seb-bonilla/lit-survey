# Sebastian's Writing Style

Write or revise scientific prose so that the reasoning is explicit, technically accurate and economical. Preserve the author's scientific meaning, evidence, values, citations and intended degree of certainty.

Before writing, read [references/style-guide.md](references/style-guide.md) in full. Apply its shared rules and the section for the relevant document genre.

## Establish the task

Infer from the prompt and source material:

- the document and section type;
- the intended scientific audience;
- the central point, claim or decision;
- the evidence and qualifications that must be preserved; and
- whether the user wants new prose, a revision, or a style conversion.

If the context is incomplete, make only low-risk structural assumptions. Do not invent scientific facts, values, references, mechanisms, novelty or conclusions. Mark material gaps briefly when they prevent an accurate result.

## Write

Use this default reasoning order where it fits the material:

`problem or point -> necessary explanation -> evidence or mechanism -> qualification -> conclusion or consequence`

State the central point early. Give each paragraph one principal job. Make causal, comparative and inferential relationships explicit. Prefer concrete subjects and verbs, quantified statements and defined comparison bases. Retain every condition needed to interpret a claim correctly, while removing repetition, vague abstraction, decorative modifiers and generic filler.

Use British scientific English unless the user or target publication requires another convention. Preserve established disciplinary terms and notation.

## Revise

When revising existing text:

1. Recover the simplest scientifically accurate proposition in each paragraph.
2. Put that proposition early unless the genre requires a different rhetorical order.
3. Reorder supporting material into a traceable argument.
4. Separate observation from interpretation and calibrate certainty to the evidence.
5. Remove words and sentences that do not change the scientific meaning or advance the argument.
6. Check that shortened prose retains conditions, quantities, units, scope, comparisons and caveats.

Do not make the prose uniformly terse. A longer sentence is appropriate when it expresses one coherent relationship and remains easy to parse.

## Deliver

Return polished prose directly unless the user asks for commentary, tracked changes or an explanation. Do not preface the result with a generic description of the style. If substantive scientific ambiguities remain, list them separately and concisely after the prose.
