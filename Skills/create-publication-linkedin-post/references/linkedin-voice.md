Observed on 3 October 2026: warm lead-author congratulations; occasional personal reflection on research; clear explanation of a technical obstacle; a concise account of the approach; diamond bullets for findings; a broader consequence; named collaborator thanks; organisation tags; a paper link and topical hashtags. The post uses a four-page PDF carousel. Public access showed its document viewer and figure content, but sign-in prevented a complete inspection of the carousel cover and remaining pages. Do not claim that all previous posts or every visual detail were inspected.

Use these structural and tonal features without recycling its wording or importing its personal anecdotes. Warmth belongs mainly in the recognition and acknowledgements. The scientific explanation should remain precise and economical, with the confidence of each claim matched to the paper. A lead-author-led introduction is a useful default, not a mandatory phrase. Do not manufacture a relationship, PhD milestone or a claim that someone made a specific contribution when the source does not establish it.

Sebastian's feedback on the Sharpe trial: the 307-word draft was too long. Prefer about 180–230 words for the complete post, preserving the warmth, principal findings, necessary qualifications and collaborator recognition. Compress background and method detail before removing scientific boundaries. Keep detailed links, tags and evidence in the accompanying notes rather than expanding the post.

Allow first-person plural, restrained enthusiasm and a few bullets or emojis, because this is public communication rather than manuscript prose. Avoid generic generated openings, inflated breakthrough language, marketing slogans and requests for engagement that Sebastian has not asked for. Keep enough explanation for a scientist outside the immediate field to understand why the findings matter.

For the Sharpe SOLMAT trial, the strongest thread is measuring where hydrogen sits in passivation stacks and showing why its bulk concentration alone does not explain passivation quality. Present the AZO hydrogen-source interpretation as suggested by these samples, not universally established. Implied open-circuit voltage is not a completed-cell voltage or efficiency.
